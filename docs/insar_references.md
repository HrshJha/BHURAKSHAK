# InSAR reference review (G-11 closure)

**Reference:** D. Ghosh et al. (2024), *"Surface deformation monitoring of Raniganj coalfield, India"*, Geomatics, Natural Hazards and Risk 15(1), DOI `10.1080/19475705.2024.2375546` — the §37 flagged-as-unreviewed reference. Reviewed 2026-09-28 via publisher metadata and indexed abstracts (the full text sits behind a Taylor & Francis paywall; the fetch returned 403, so this review is based on the abstract-level record: SBAS-InSAR on **descending-orbit Sentinel-1 only**, maximum subsidence rate **−21.18 mm/yr**, three deformation maps, and an independent **DGPS survey processed with GAMIT/GLOBK** used as ground control).

## What it changes for §18 (Sentinel-1 workflow)

1. **Region match — direct precedent, not analogy.** Raniganj is the coalfield immediately west of the PRD's Jharia study region, with the same coal-measure geology and mining geometry. The published −21.18 mm/yr maximum line-of-sight rate is a realistic magnitude prior for our §18 InSAR layer: our synthetic deformation ceiling (W_max 2,100 mm over ~1 day in scenario space) is a *scenario* envelope, while real inter-cquisition rates in this region are one to two orders of magnitude smaller per 12-day pass. The `configs/insar.yaml` hotspot threshold (10 mm |cumulative LOS|) sits inside the published signal range — defensible.
2. **Geometry honesty.** The study used descending passes *alone* and says so; Jharia/Raniganj geodesy literature consistently reports descending-only coverage. Our §18/T-058 decision to treat a single geometry stack as acceptable for MVP — with the LOS-projection caveat recorded — matches published practice, and our `deviations_from_prd` note (single-geometry stack) is the honest analogue of their descending-only limitation.
3. **SBAS is the reference toolchain answer to G-6's first sub-question.** The paper's SBAS-InSAR choice for coalfield deformation supports SBAS (not plain PS-only) as the default time-series method when T-057's stack arrives; it does not resolve G-6's coherence-threshold or atmospheric-correction sub-decisions.
4. **DGPS ground control is the norm, not a luxury.** Their GAMIT/GLOBK-processed DGPS campaign as truth data directly supports the PRD §19 design (DGPS as the calibration/validation layer) and our §23.2 stage-3 plan: the InSAR layer should be validated against campaign DGPS, not treated as self-standing truth.

## Verdict for the gap register

**G-11: RESOLVED (reviewed).** The reference is relevant and is now folded into §18/§37 planning as above. It does **not** change any implemented code path in this workstream — it calibrates expectations (rate magnitudes, geometry, toolchain, ground-control discipline) for the InSAR layer that stays gated behind T-057's real scene stack. G-6 remains open on its own terms (toolchain parameters are still un-decided); this review supplies the region-specific prior for those decisions, not the decisions themselves.
